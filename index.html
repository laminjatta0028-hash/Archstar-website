Understanding Architecture

What is Architecture?

Architecture is the art and science of designing buildings and structures. It combines creativity, technical knowledge, and understanding of human needs to create spaces that are both functional and beautiful.

Design Functionality Aesthetics

Architecture vs Related Fields

Architect

  • Designs buildings and structures
  • Focuses on form, function, and safety
  • Requires professional license
  • Works on construction documents
  • Oversees construction process

Interior Designer

  • Designs interior spaces
  • Focuses on aesthetics and functionality
  • Selects furniture and finishes
  • Creates mood and atmosphere
  • Works within existing structures

Urban Planner

  • Plans cities and communities
  • Focuses on land use and infrastructure
  • Considers transportation and zoning
  • Works with government policies
  • Thinks at neighborhood/city scale

Beginner Guides

01

How to Start Architecture in High School

1

Develop Drawing Skills

Practice sketching buildings, perspective drawing, and technical drafting. Take art classes and carry a sketchbook everywhere.

2

Study Math and Physics

Focus on geometry, trigonometry, and basic physics. These subjects are crucial for understanding structural principles.

3

Explore Architecture Around You

Visit buildings, take photos, analyze what makes spaces work. Start noticing details in buildings you encounter daily.

4

Build Simple Models

Use cardboard, foam board, or digital tools to create scale models of buildings or spaces you design.

Essential Skills Before University

Technical Drawing

Learn hand drafting, perspective, and orthographic projection

Spatial Thinking

Develop ability to visualize 3D spaces from 2D drawings

Design Fundamentals

Understand balance, proportion, rhythm, and harmony

Digital Tools

Start with SketchUp, AutoCAD basics, or Revit fundamentals

Architectural History

Study major movements and iconic buildings throughout history

Communication

Practice presenting ideas clearly through words and visuals

Portfolio Development

Freehand Sketching

Develop your ability to quickly capture ideas and observations. Practice drawing buildings, spaces, and details from life.

Practice Tips:

  • Carry a sketchbook daily
  • Start with basic shapes
  • Focus on proportion first
  • Add details gradually
  • Use different line weights

Perspective Drawing

Master one-point, two-point, and three-point perspective to create convincing architectural drawings.

Key Concepts:

  • Horizon line placement
  • Vanishing points
  • Scale and proportion
  • Adding people for scale
  • Shadows and depth

Technical Drawing

Learn to create precise plans, sections, and elevations that communicate your design ideas clearly.

Essential Elements:

  • Line weights and types
  • Dimensioning standards
  • Scale selection
  • Notation and symbols
  • Sheet layout

Cardboard Models

Create study models using simple materials. Perfect for exploring form and space quickly.

Materials Needed:

  • Cardboard or foam board
  • Cutting mat and hobby knife
  • Metal ruler
  • Glue or double-sided tape
  • Pencils and markers

Recycled Materials

Use everyday items to create unique architectural models. Sustainable and creative approach.

Creative Materials:

  • Plastic containers
  • Cardboard tubes
  • Wire and mesh
  • Fabric scraps
  • Wood scraps

Digital Models

Learn 3D modeling software to create precise digital representations of your designs.

Software Options:

  • SketchUp (Beginner)
  • Blender (Free)
  • Rhino (Advanced)
  • Revit (Professional)
  • 3ds Max (Rendering)

Digital Drawing

Use tablets and software to create precise architectural drawings and presentations.

Recommended Tools:

  • Adobe Photoshop
  • Adobe Illustrator
  • Procreate (iPad)
  • AutoCAD
  • Concepts App

3D Visualization

Create stunning renderings and visualizations of your architectural designs.

Rendering Tips:

  • Learn lighting basics
  • Understand materials
  • Add context and landscaping
  • Include people for scale
  • Post-process in Photoshop

Diagrams & Analysis

Develop skills in creating clear diagrams that explain your design thinking.

Diagram Types:

  • Circulation diagrams
  • Sun/shadow studies
  • Program analysis
  • Structural diagrams
  • Conceptual diagrams

Insights from the Field

Sarah Chen

3rd Year Architecture Student

What's the biggest challenge in architecture school?
Time management! You'll have multiple projects running simultaneously, and learning to prioritize and manage your time effectively is crucial. The workload is intense, but it's also incredibly rewarding.
Any advice for high school students?
Start observing buildings around you. Take photos, make sketches, and think about why certain spaces feel good. Also, practice your drawing skills - both technical and freehand. Don't worry about being perfect, just keep practicing!

Michael Rodriguez

Licensed Architect, 10 years experience

What makes a great architect?
A great architect combines creativity with problem-solving skills. You need to be able to think spatially, understand how people use spaces, and balance aesthetic vision with practical constraints like budget and building codes.
How important is math in architecture?
You need a solid foundation in math, especially geometry and basic algebra. But don't let that scare you - most complex calculations are done by computers now. What's more important is understanding spatial relationships and being able to think three-dimensionally.

Emma Thompson

Architecture Professor

What do you look for in student portfolios?
I look for creativity, curiosity, and potential. Show me your thought process, not just final products. Include sketches, studies, experiments - even failed attempts. We want to see how you think and solve problems, not just pretty pictures.
What's the future of architecture?
Sustainability and technology integration are huge. We're designing buildings that generate their own energy, use smart materials, and adapt to their environment. Also, there's more focus on human wellness and creating spaces that promote health and well-being.
